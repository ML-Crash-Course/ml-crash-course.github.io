# Writing guidelines

These rules apply to every piece of text a learner sees: lesson prose, hints, captions, exercise feedback, button labels, headings and summaries. Code identifiers are exempt.

## Tone

- Neutral scientific prose. Calm, precise, no hype.
- Define every term at its first use.
- Vary sentence length. Do not end a paragraph with a punchy one-line summary.
- No announcing or meta sentences ("In this section we will...", "Let's dive in", "What this session covers").
- At most one "!" per section.
- UI feedback stays calm, for example "Correct. +10 XP".

## Punctuation and characters

- No semicolons. Split the sentence or use a conjunction.
- No em dashes and no en dashes. Use a comma, a colon, parentheses or a new sentence.
- Keyboard characters only: "->" for arrows, straight quotes, "..." for an ellipsis.
- No emoji unless explicitly requested.
- Headings in sentence case, without a full stop.

## Headings and labels

- Headings name a neutral topic as a noun phrase: "The chain rule", "Saddle points", "Learning rate and curvature".
- No questions, no "Where X comes from", "Why X", "How X", "What X", no imperatives ("Find the summit"), no colons, no wordplay.
- No all-caps labels, eyebrows or kickers above a heading. A heading stands on its own.

## Sentence patterns that sound machine-written

- The colon reveal: "X has one property: Y", "The idea is simple: Y". Write two plain sentences or one clause with a verb.
- Aphorisms and mirrored phrasing: "one unit is one fold", "the grid moves, the vector does not".
- Drama and personification: survive, trapped, catapulted, pops up, the missing ingredient, quietly, magic.
- Intensifiers: exactly, literally, really, actually, simply, just.
- "Watch X happen", "Notice that", "Note that". Describe what the figure shows instead.
- Reflexive lists of three. Use as many items as there are.
- Recaps that repeat every step. A recap states the main results in a few sentences.

## Banned words and phrases

- "readout" (say "the values below the plot", "the numbers", or name the quantity)
- "by hand"
- "rather than", and the construction "not X but Y"
- "In practice", "In effect", "That said", "To be clear"
- notably, crucially, importantly, key, robust, genuinely, essentially, fundamentally, leverage, delve, striking, remarkable

## Math

- Inline math with `\\( ... \\)`, display math with `\\[ ... \\]`. Never `$`.
